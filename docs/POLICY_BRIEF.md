# Antenatal care in Ethiopia: where women are lost, and what the data says to do

**Policy brief for the Federal Ministry of Health**
Based on the Ethiopia Demographic and Health Survey 2024-25

---

## The five findings

1. **Most women reach antenatal care, but most do not complete it.** 78%
   receive at least one contact, but only 19% reach the eight the WHO
   recommends. The biggest loss is between the fourth contact and the eighth,
   not at the door.

2. **Regions with the same coverage are failing for opposite reasons.** In
   Somali two-thirds of women never reach care; in Central Ethiopia almost
   all do but few continue past four contacts. One national programme cannot
   serve both.

3. **Among women who already attend, poverty decides who finishes.** Of women
   who reached four contacts, the richest fifth are two and a half times as
   likely to reach eight as the poorest fifth. These women want care; the cost
   of repeated visits is what stops them.

4. **The Tigray war cut women off from care entirely.** During the war about
   one pregnant woman in five in Tigray received no antenatal care, against
   one in thirteen after the ceasefire. Access has recovered; completion has
   not.

5. **A handful of facts about a woman carry most of the prediction.**
   Household wealth and region lead on every outcome; whether the pregnancy
   was wanted matters for staying in care. For eight contacts, seven or eight
   questions predict as well as 21 or 88; for reaching care, the fuller set
   of survey information is still the most accurate.

---

## 1. The problem is holding women, not reaching them

| | Share of women with a recent birth |
|---|---|
| Received any antenatal care | 78% |
| Four or more contacts | 52% (53% in the official 24-month indicator) |
| Eight or more contacts | 19% |

Of women who start antenatal care, two-thirds reach four contacts. Of those
who reach four, **only about a third reach eight**. Nationally, about one
woman in three reaches four contacts and then stops. That is a larger group
than the women who never receive care at all (22%).

**Implication.** Measured by the WHO's eight-contact standard, the largest
gain is in *retention*: keeping women who already attend coming back.

---

## 2. Each region needs a different programme

Where each region loses women (share of all women with a recent birth):

| Region | Reach 8+ | Never receive care | Start, stop before 4 | Reach 4, stop before 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Somali | 4% | **66%** | 22% | 8% |
| South Ethiopia | 8% | 25% | 30% | **37%** |
| Sidama | 9% | 15% | **44%** | 31% |
| Central Ethiopia | 10% | 14% | 28% | **48%** |
| Tigray | 12% | 13% | 34% | **42%** |
| Addis Ababa | 66% | 1% | 5% | 28% |

The same pattern holds using only births in the last two years, the official
indicator window.

| If a region's biggest loss is... | ...it needs | Regions |
|---|---|---|
| Women never reaching care | **Outreach:** bring the service to women it does not yet reach | Somali (66%); nearly a third of women in Oromia and Afar |
| Women stopping before four | **Early engagement:** hold women through the first half of pregnancy | Sidama |
| Women stopping between four and eight | **Retention:** follow-up, reminders, lower cost of each return visit | Central Ethiopia, Tigray, South Ethiopia, Gambella |

**The ceiling is reachable in Ethiopia.** Addis Ababa keeps 95% of women who
start through to four contacts and 70% from four to eight. That is an
Ethiopian result, not an imported benchmark.

**Afar outperforms its circumstances.** Given its population's wealth,
education and household profile, Afar attains far more than expected: about
15 percentage points above prediction for eight contacts, in both birth
windows. Whatever is working there is worth understanding and copying. The
survey cannot say what it is; the Ministry's own records can.

---

## 3. For women who already attend, cost of repeat visits is the barrier

Among women who **already reached four contacts**, the share who went on to
eight:

| | Continue from 4 to 8 |
|---|---|
| Richest fifth | 52% |
| Middle | 31% |
| Poorest fifth | 20% |
| Urban | 45% |
| Rural | 30% |

Every woman in this table has already shown she values antenatal care by
attending four times. What differs is the cost of returning: transport paid
eight times instead of four, days of work lost, childcare.

**Implication.** Reducing the cost of each additional visit for poorer women
who are already engaged (transport support, visits aligned with other
services, care closer to home) targets a narrower and cheaper problem than
general demand creation. The survey's household health-expenditure module
could size this cost directly; it has not yet been analysed here.

---

## 4. The Tigray war and recovery

Comparing Tigrayan women pregnant during the war (November 2020 to November
2022) with those who conceived after the ceasefire, and subtracting the
change in peaceful regions over the same period:

| Tigray | During the war | After the ceasefire | Change beyond other regions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Received no antenatal care | 20% | 8% | **13 points fewer** |
| Four or more contacts | 46% | 59% | **14 points more** |
| Eight or more contacts | 10% | 12% | no clear change |

**What happened.** The war stopped women reaching care at all. It did not
mainly make them drop out partway. After the ceasefire, access recovered,
and Tigray now does better than its circumstances predict at getting women
into care. But returning women mostly stopped at four contacts, so Tigray has
become a retention problem like Central Ethiopia.

**For crisis planning:** in conflict, the first loss is access to any care.
Keeping even a basic antenatal contact available (mobile outreach, health
extension workers) protects the rung that collapses first.

These figures describe women who were interviewed. Women displaced or killed,
and areas too insecure to survey, are missing, so the true wartime loss is
probably larger. The Amhara conflict since August 2023 falls on the most
recent births and could not yet be assessed the same way.

---

## 5. What predicts attainment, and what that means for targeting

Across all 88 candidate characteristics in the survey, the ones that
repeatedly carry the prediction of eight-contact attainment are:

- **household wealth**, used as the continuous DHS wealth score, not the
  five-group quintile. It is chosen first for every outcome;
- **region**, chosen for every outcome;
- **whether the pregnancy was wanted at the time**, for four and eight
  contacts but not for reaching care at all: it is about staying in care;
- **years of schooling**, and **access to a health post** (especially living
  next to one) for eight contacts;
- less consistently: the woman's say in household decisions, her occupation,
  and her number of children.

**How many questions are needed depends on the goal.** For eight contacts,
**seven or eight** questions (wealth, region, pregnancy intention, the
woman's work and say in household decisions, and access to a health post)
predict as well as a 21-question or 88-question set on women the models never
saw. For reaching care and for four contacts, **the full survey information
is the most accurate**, because many small factors (distance, transport,
media, household amenities) each add a little. The practical choice:

- where every question has a cost (a registration form, a screening at first
  contact), **seven or eight questions capture the value for completing
  care**;
- where the data already exist (DHS, household registers), use all of it.

**Two cautions for using this:**

- **Prediction is not cause.** Wealth and region predict attainment; that
  does not mean handing out money would produce it. Of the strong
  predictors, **health-post access is the one a health service directly
  controls.**
- **The tools are accurate enough to direct attention across a population,
  not to judge an individual woman.** They rank women correctly about 78% of
  the time. Use them to decide *where* and *for whom* to invest, not to deny
  anyone care.

**An equity question for the Ministry, not the model.** A woman's first
language predicts attainment strongly, mainly because it stands in for region
and ethnicity. Removing it costs almost nothing in accuracy. Whether a tool
should use it is a policy decision: defensible for directing outreach to
under-served groups, not for scoring an individual lower. It should be
decided deliberately, not left in by accident.

---

## Recommendations

1. **Target by where each region loses women, not by its headline
   coverage.** Outreach where women never arrive; retention where they arrive
   and stop.
2. **Make retention the national priority for the eight-contact standard.**
   One woman in three reaches four contacts and stops, more than the one in
   five who never start.
3. **Lower the cost of each return visit** for poorer women already in care.
4. **Learn from Afar and Addis Ababa.** Both show what is achievable under
   Ethiopian conditions; find out what they do.
5. **Protect first contact in conflict and crisis.** It is the rung that
   collapses first, and it recovered in Tigray once access was restored.
6. **Keep targeting tools explainable, and short where questions are
   costly.** Four well-measured characteristics capture most of the value;
   add more only where the data already exist.
7. **Pair the survey with facility records.** The DHS shows *where* women are
   lost; facility data are needed to learn *why*: waiting times, stockouts,
   staffing, how women are treated.

---

## About the evidence

- **Source.** Ethiopia DHS 2024-25. 6,893 women whose most recent pregnancy
  has a recorded antenatal care count; 5,037 in the official 24-month window.
  All figures are survey-weighted to represent the country.
- **Checked.** The analysis reproduces the EDHS report's own four-contact
  figures (national 53.0% against 53.0%; every region within 1.5 points).
- **Descriptive vs predictive.** The regional cascade and the continuation
  figures are counts and need no model. They are the sturdiest findings. The
  predictor findings come from models and describe associations, not causes.
- **Not covered.** No facility data; the outcome is the woman's recall; one
  survey round only.
